01 / The method

Follow the site, then put the records in order.

A tax request alone cannot tell a neighborhood what is coming. Add a dated site plan, power decision and jobs commitment, and the questions become sharper. We still have to check whether those records describe the same project and phase.

RESOLVE

Which site and which project?

Match the applicant, affiliate, parcel and power request. If the link is uncertain, say so.

SEQUENCE

What happened, and when?

Put the application, vote, permit, amendment and correction in order. Keep the original act distinct from a copy of it.

TRANSLATE

Who can use the answer?

Give residents, workers, public buyers and other readers the facts they need, the uncertainty that matters, and the next document to seek.

Why the combined view matters: it can show how a public subsidy, a power request and a workforce promise relate over time. Why the proof rule matters: copied notices and Internet clues cannot stand in for another independent official decision.

What can each source actually tell us?

A record you can read may still have limits on collection or reuse. An open-source tool does not make its inputs open data. Check each source before building on it.

04 / One case, different work

The same record can answer different public questions.

A resident may ask about the next hearing. A worker may ask whether promised jobs became enforceable terms. A public buyer may ask who controls a service and how it can be repaired. Each answer needs a source and a next step.

Resident brief

What is proposed near us?

An IDA packet names a site. A land-use notice describes a plan. A power filing may affect it. Put the original acts in date order, then show residents what has been requested, decided and left open.

Next public record to request

The full site plan and the lead agency’s decision.

These are sample openings, not findings about a real site or a finished automated product.

How to read a Docket finding

A finding should let someone open the original act, locate the site and phase, see the date and identify a conflict. If the next needed record is missing, the brief should say so.

Packet inspection has a boundary

BGP, DNS and traceroute can point us toward a question; they cannot show packets inside a candidate facility. Published packet traces describe the place they were collected. Operator flows, packet inspection and service logs require permission and separate governance.
