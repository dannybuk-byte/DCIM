Shape the bargain.

A tax deal, a power filing and a planning notice may describe the same data-center project at different times and in different terms. Data Center Docket would bring those public records together, show what they actually establish, and help workers and neighbors ask better questions before the next decision is made. Public network clues can help us find a lead; they cannot confirm a facility on their own.

One place. Different questions.

The same public case can help a resident ask what was promised, a worker ask who gets trained, or a buyer ask what a contract must guarantee. The answer depends on the record and on who has authority to act.

How a public case would work · proposed

From a clue to a question worth asking

A filing suggests a new load. A local tax agreement names a project. Do they point to the same place and phase? The tool would line up the original records, test their independence and show what is still missing. If the match fails, that result stays visible too.

01 / FINDFollow the public money, permits and powerStart with IDA tax deals, DEC/SEQR notices, municipal boards, PSC/DPS filings and relevant utility or NYISO records.
02 / CHECKUse clues to find the right recordCorporate, parcel, BGP, RIPE and DNS data can suggest where to look. They cannot confirm a facility or expose private traffic.
03 / VERIFYMatch the place, phase and claimKeep the dates and versions. Treat copies from one agency as one origin. Require two eligible independent official acts for facility confirmation.
04 / DECIDEPut the right question in the right handsShow the source, the conflict and the next record to seek. A resident, worker or public buyer decides what to ask or do.

This is a proposed workflow. No live official-record-to-interface detector is established; the verified real-site corpus is zero, and no measured lead-time advantage is established.

Where this could matter

Start with the decision, then build the view.

A neighbor checking a promise, a buyer writing a service contract and a worker testing repair access do not need the same report. Open a route to see the question, a small test and the people who could carry it forward. Each route needs its own evidence and decision-maker.

01

Host community & labor

Which promises can a community actually hold to?

The question A town hears that a data center will bring jobs and investment. Which public agreement commits to what, for whom and by when? A tax deal, SEQR notice and power filing may describe different phases. Residents need to see the promise; workers need to know the employer, paid training path and job terms; oversight staff need the missing record.

A first test Build one phase-aware timeline and obligation ledger from original acts. Include a false site match and a copied agency notice so reviewers can see where the claim should stop. Requested megawatts are not energized capacity, and announced jobs are not delivered jobs.

Who acts next Residents choose the obligation to check. Workers define the job question. Public staff seek the agreement or amendment that could settle it.

02

Computational Antitrust

Where do shared intermediaries narrow real choice?

The question Several public bids may depend on the same pricing reference, cloud platform, assessor or access rule. Does that leave buyers with a meaningful choice? A pattern is a reason to investigate, not evidence of collusion. Common costs, quality, capacity and lawful standards may explain it.

A first test Map one set of public tenders, awards and changing clauses. Let a competition researcher test alternatives and a buyer test substitution. Keep sensitive rival bids out of a shared public pool; legal conclusions belong to qualified counsel and economists.

Who acts next The buyer names the choice it needs. Competition, repair and standards reviewers test different bottlenecks. The buyer decides whether a lawful contract term would improve exit.

03

CAP policy attention

When did an issue enter the agenda, and when did policy change?

The question A topic can draw attention long before a bill, permit or contract changes anyone’s obligations. CAP classifications could help a policy analyst trace data-center, AI, energy or labor attention, while the original text shows what officials actually did. A CAP code is not a permit or proof of causation.

A first test Check the codebook, dataset version and rights for one jurisdiction. Link a coded item to its original hearing or bill, with uncertain matches visible. A complete CAP row-level corpus and verified AI/data-center subset were not obtained for this project; a live overlay remains unproved.

Who acts next A policy scholar checks the classification. Legislative staff select the agenda question. A local reviewer separately checks the operative site record.

04

DPI & municipal procurement

Could a public service survive a supplier change?

The question A downloadable file is not enough if the next team cannot diagnose, update, recover or transfer a public service. For one municipal purchase, what can a qualified receiving team actually do, and who pays for the skills to do it? A data-center site belongs in this case only if a real contract or public act connects it.

A first test Follow one service from tender through award, amendment and handoff. Pair each portability clause with an authorized task, an exception and a receiving-side test. Compare the result with the incumbent supplier’s support.

Who acts next Service users name the failure they fear. The buyer writes the requirement. Technical and worker reviewers test it. Oversight checks whether the contract delivered.

05

Insurance & assurance

What does it take to restore an interrupted service?

The question A resilience promise is hard to judge without knowing who can diagnose a fault, obtain parts, exercise credentials and bring the service back. The public record may show a disclosed commitment, but it cannot reveal a private incident or establish an insured loss.

A first test With separate operator permission, time one defined fault drill from detection through authorization, repair and restoration. Let a risk engineer define the loss and exclusions, workers test safe paid authority, and buyers check continuity. Compare integrated support and other plausible paths; a faster step alone does not prove lower premiums or coverage.

Who acts next Worker task → permitted function test → operator observation → qualified loss analysis → buyer or insurer decision under the actual contract.

06

Repair, standards & workforce

Can a qualified worker really use the promised interface?

The question An “open” interface can still leave a technician without the credentials, parts, firmware or service rights needed to finish a lawful repair. The answer turns on the exact product and version, the task and the worker’s safe, paid authority. A badge or published specification does not settle it.

A first test Ask an independent receiving team to perform one authorized task. Record the version, access term, failure point and a case where integrated supplier support works better. Keep security exceptions and legal scope for qualified review.

Who acts next Repairers name the barrier. Standards teams test the function. Workers define training and authority. Buyers decide what belongs in acceptance and remedy terms.

07

Compute markets & labor capital

What backs a compute contract—and who bears the risk?

The question A GPU-hour benchmark, reserved capacity, a financed building and a delivered service are different things. Which contract actually links them, and what happens if capacity or service fails? Buyers, lenders, workers, labor-capital reviewers and competition researchers each have a different stake and decision right.

A first test Trace one licensed benchmark into an adopting contract, with a fallback term and a service-failure scenario. Name the operator, capacity condition and any public promise only where documents connect them. Exposure alone does not establish pension control, public ownership or insurance savings.

Who acts next The benchmark specialist checks the reference. The buyer tests delivery and exit. Labor-capital and risk reviewers trace their actual authority and downside.

08

Place & civic storytelling

Can people see the project in its real local context?

The question A resident should be able to stand on a map and see which phase is proposed, which hearing or power action applies, what jobs were promised and where to read the source. A compelling rendering can also mislead: proximity does not prove a physical cable, live packet path or partnership.

A first test Build an accessible 2D map and timeline for one contested case, with original-record cards and a text/table view. Ask a second reviewer to find the contrary record. Add 3D or AR only when the geometry is sourced and the device and accessibility questions are tested.

Who acts next Residents choose the place-based question. Planners verify geometry. Reporters check the labels. Workers and educators test the claimed opportunity.

09

Authorized traffic & workloads

With permission, which service events waste work?

The question Public BGP, RIPE and DNS clues cannot show an operator’s private task graph, traffic or exact energy use. With separate consent, a service team could examine a specific retry, cache miss, cancellation or recovery event. A blanket “slop” label would also catch useful research, redundancy and creativity.

A first test Use a synthetic or permitted event with task, version and time. Measure useful outcome, resource cost and false blocks, with appeal and minimal collection. Keep restricted logs out of the Public Docket and exclude individual worker productivity scoring.

Who acts next Users define useful work. Service and privacy teams bound the event. Workers review labor effects. The operator decides on a reversible control.

10

Public contribution & circular hardware

When does public support become usable public capacity?

The question A subsidy, retired server or donated room does not by itself become a library service or community-owned compute. Who holds the rights, pays the maintainer, supports the learner and handles failure? A public contribution does not automatically create public title or a free supply of capacity.

A first test Follow one contribution into an actual service and compare the full cost with ordinary procurement. Include equipment condition, safe refurbishment, access, training, disposal, maintenance and exit. Test a case where a proposed commons creates a new gatekeeper.

Who acts next A community institution defines the service. Cooperative and worker teams specify governance and upkeep. A buyer or funder tests the cost and exit.

11

Workforce disclosure

Do workforce claims line up with public disclosures?

The question An employer may describe AI and staffing one way in an earnings call and another way in a WARN notice. That difference warrants a precise question; it does not prove why any individual worker lost a job. A construction signal likewise says nothing by itself about hiring.

A first test Compare original filings for one employer, period and claim. Resolve entity and timing, test alternative explanations, preserve correction and protect any worker testimony. This disclosure inquiry has its own evidence rule; it cannot borrow the two-act facility confirmation test.

Who acts next Workers frame the question. Researchers align the filings. Journalists or oversight staff check it and offer a correction path.

The full issue pitches include source and capability limits and an adverse test for each route.

A shared record can support different decisions

Keep the original document close. Say precisely what it shows. Make room for a contrary record. Then ask the person with authority what they need next. A link between a facility, a contract or a policy agenda has to be shown, not assumed.

01 / FINDShow the originalKeep the issuing body, date, version and exact place or phase.
02 / SAYMake one clear claimName what the record establishes and what it leaves open.
03 / TESTTry to disprove itCheck source independence, conflicts and better explanations.
04 / ACTMake the next moveRequest a record, test a function or write a better term.
Public research and private operations need different permissions. The proposed Public Docket uses original public records and support-only OSINT clues. A future traffic or workload view would need the operator’s separate authorization and its own safeguards. Neither view is a live integration today.

How one bridge could work

Start with a repair task. A technician shows what they cannot do. A standards team tests the interface. Workers set safe, paid training terms. A public buyer decides whether the tested function belongs in the contract.

Or start with a local promise. Residents choose a commitment to check. The original agreement shows who is bound. Oversight asks whether it happened.

These are proposed routes for collaboration. A named organization or audience has not endorsed or agreed to participate.
